Segmentation Granularity and LLM Reranking

Master's thesis, University of Southern Maine, 2026

  • Benchmarked a four stage NLP pipeline in Python for legal argument retrieval, comparing segmentation strategies (document, paragraph, sentence, proposition) against transformer models and aggregation methods across 60+ configurations, so the effect of each design choice could be isolated.
  • Built a multi stage retrieval and reranking architecture using BM25 for candidate retrieval, fine tuned cross encoders for classification, and zero shot DeepSeek reranking.
  • Found that a strong instruction tuned LLM outperformed a fine tuned cross encoder baseline without any task specific training, which matters for teams weighing annotation budget against inference cost.

Family Planning Register, Ghana

Team lead 2025 · Team of 5

The problem. Nurses in Ghana tracked family planning services on paper registers that had to be transcribed by hand into monthly Ministry of Health reports. It was slow, error prone, and painful every time the annual form changed.

What we built. A Java 17 desktop system that digitizes the register: patient records, inventory tracking, automated monthly report generation, and data export. I led a team of five, splitting the work into patient side and inventory side services with a clean domain model between them, and ran weekly sprints across three iterations from prototype to integrated UI.

The hard part was conforming to Ministry of Health reporting standards. The output had to match an externally defined format we could not negotiate, so the report layer had to absorb schema changes without the domain model moving. Building to a spec set by someone else turned out to be the most realistic part of the project.

Early Detection of Anorexia Signals in Text

SVM vs. DistilBERT

A text classification pipeline comparing a classical TF-IDF and SVM approach against a fine tuned DistilBERT transformer for detecting early signals of anorexia in user generated text. Evaluated on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1, plus the early risk metrics that matter for this task specifically: ERDE@N, latency, and latency weighted F1, which penalize a model for being right too late.

A parallel variant tested the effect of stopword and common word removal on transformer performance, which is often assumed to help and frequently does not.
